THE PREMISE02 / 16
§1  DEFINITION

Phases run once, in order, with no overlap

Each phase must be fully complete and formally approved before the next begins. The output of one phase is the sole input to the next.

  • Progress flows one direction only
  • Documents carry knowledge between phases
  • A signature gates every transition

The V-Model pairs every phase with its test

Writing a requirement forces you to define how it will be verified. If you cannot describe the test, the requirement is not written clearly enough.

VARIANTS10 / 16
§9  ADVANTAGES

What the model buys you

  • Estimable. Fixed scope makes Gantt charts, critical paths, and budget commitments possible
  • Auditable. Traceability from requirement to test is mandatory evidence in regulated industries
  • Durable knowledge. When people leave, the documentation stays
  • Low customer load. Stakeholders appear at gates, not daily standups
  • Specialist efficiency. Staff move between projects as phases shift
ADVANTAGES11 / 16
§10  DISADVANTAGES

What it costs you

  • Rigid. Every change after the freeze needs formal approval and repricing
  • Late visibility. On an 18-month project, nothing runs for 15 of them
  • Back-loaded risk. Wrong assumptions surface when the money is spent
  • False premise. Users understand their needs after using something, not before
  • Documentation theatre. Mandated documents get written to satisfy the process, then go stale
  • Idle capacity. Testers wait months; analysts idle at the end
  • Silent gaps. A requirement nobody raised stays invisible until UAT
DISADVANTAGES12 / 16
§11  FIT

Building versus discovering

✓

Use it when

  • Requirements are stable and well understood
  • The technology holds no unknowns
  • Compliance evidence is required
  • The contract is fixed-price, fixed-scope
  • A production defect is expensive or dangerous
BUILDING · THE ANSWER IS KNOWN
✕

Avoid it when

  • Requirements are unclear or certain to move
  • The product needs continuous user experimentation
  • The technology is new and unproven
  • Time to market is the binding constraint
  • The work is exploratory or research

§12  COMPARISON

Waterfall and Agile answer different questions

CRITERIONWATERFALLAGILE
REQUIREMENTSLocked at the outsetContinuously evolving
DELIVERYOnce, at the endEvery sprint
DOCUMENTATIONCentral and mandatorySufficient only
CUSTOMERPresent at phase gatesPresent continuously
TESTINGAfter implementationAlongside development
RISKSurfaces late, all at onceSurfaces early, in pieces
WHAT IS FIXEDScope; time and cost flexTime and cost; scope flexes

Not right and wrong — two tools for two classes of problem. Most organisations now run a hybrid: Waterfall governance around Agile execution.

COMPARISON14 / 16
§13  EVIDENCE

Same method, opposite outcomes

Blood glucose meter firmware

  • Requirements fixed by medical standards
  • Certification demands full traceability
  • Embedded software, no remote updates

On schedule, certified first attempt.

Retail e-commerce platform, 20 months

  • Month 12 — market shifts, change rejected
  • Month 17 — architecture fails load testing
  • Month 19 — UAT rejects the payment flow

Seven months late, 40% over budget.

The deciding difference was not scale or budget. It was uncertainty. All three failures in the second case were detectable in month one with a working prototype.

EVIDENCE15 / 16
§14  CONCLUSION

Uncertainty decides the model, not fashion

Where uncertainty is low, planning holds its value. Where it is high, a plan decays faster than it can be executed. A good engineer diagnoses the context before choosing the method.
